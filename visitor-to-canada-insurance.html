Visitor to Canada insurance: what to know before they land

Why visitor insurance is necessary

Provincial health insurance — OHIP in Ontario, and its equivalents elsewhere — generally does not cover visitors, tourists, or people on a temporary visa who aren't yet permanent residents. That gap is the entire reason visitor insurance exists. A single emergency room visit or hospital stay without coverage can cost tens of thousands of dollars out of pocket in Canada, which is the single biggest reason families buy a policy before a parent or grandparent's trip.

What it typically covers

Visitor insurance plans are built for emergencies, not routine care. Typical coverage includes emergency medical treatment, hospitalization, emergency doctor visits, prescription drugs tied to a covered emergency, and often emergency dental resulting from an accident.

Pre-existing conditions are the most common point of confusion. Most plans require a condition to be "stable" for a defined period before the trip — often somewhere in the range of 90 to 180 days, though this varies by insurer and policy. Always check the specific wording of the policy you're buying rather than assuming a number.

How much coverage to buy

Plans are usually sold in tiers — commonly $25,000, $50,000, $100,000, or $150,000+ of coverage. Higher coverage costs more, but it matters: a serious event like surgery or an ICU stay can easily exceed $50,000–$100,000 in Canada. Many advisors recommend not under-buying coverage just to shave a small amount off the premium — the gap between a $50,000 and $100,000 policy is usually much smaller in dollars than the gap in actual protection.

Deductibles: lower premium vs. more risk

Many visitor insurance plans let you choose a deductible — commonly $0, $500, $1,000, or $2,500. A higher deductible lowers the premium, but means more out-of-pocket cost if a claim actually happens. It's a trade-off worth thinking through against the traveller's own health history and how long the trip is.

Who should buy it

  • Parents or grandparents visiting on a Visitor Visa or eTA.
  • International students' visiting family.
  • New immigrants in their initial waiting period before provincial health coverage kicks in — many provinces apply a waiting period (often around three months) before new permanent residents are covered.
  • Anyone travelling to Canada from abroad without Canadian provincial coverage.

Buy before the flight, not after

Most plans require the policy to be purchased and in effect before the person travels, or very shortly after landing. Many also exclude claims related to conditions that weren't stable before the policy started — so timing the purchase correctly is part of making the coverage actually work.

Visitor insurance vs. Super Visa insurance

Super Visa insurance is a specific, government-mandated type of visitor insurance tied to the Super Visa program for parents and grandparents, with its own minimum coverage rules. General visitor insurance, covered in this article, applies to any visitor and is more flexible on coverage amount and terms. If you're specifically bringing a parent or grandparent on a Super Visa, see our dedicated Super Visa insurance guide for the exact requirements.

Frequently Asked

Visitor insurance, answered.

Does OHIP cover visitors to Canada?+
No — provincial health plans like OHIP generally do not cover visitors, tourists, or people without permanent resident or health-card status.
How much visitor insurance coverage do I need?+
It depends on budget and risk tolerance, but many advisors suggest not going below $50,000–$100,000 given how quickly Canadian hospital costs can add up for a serious emergency.
Are pre-existing conditions covered?+
Usually only if the condition was "stable" for a defined period before the trip — this varies by policy, so it's worth reviewing the specific wording before buying.
When should I buy visitor insurance?+
Before departure, or as soon as possible after arrival. Buying early avoids gaps, and most insurers require the policy to start before or very close to the travel date.
Can new immigrants use visitor insurance?+
Yes — it's commonly used to bridge the waiting period many provinces apply before new permanent residents become eligible for provincial health coverage.
What's the difference between visitor insurance and Super Visa insurance?+
Super Visa insurance is a specific, government-mandated type of visitor insurance for the Super Visa program with its own minimum coverage rules. General visitor insurance is more flexible and covers any visitor.
This article is educational and general in nature, and not personalized insurance advice. Policy terms, exclusions, and provincial health-coverage waiting periods vary by insurer and province — review the specific policy before purchasing.
