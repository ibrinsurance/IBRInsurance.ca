Why so many parents and grandparents in Canada end up with no coverage at all

By Rattan Amol Taggar Updated Oct 21, 2026 6 min read

A pattern we see constantly

A family sponsors or hosts parents or grandparents in Canada — sometimes permanently, often on a Super Visa — and insurance only comes up for the mandatory visitor medical coverage the visa requires. What rarely gets discussed is everything else: final expenses, outstanding debts back home, or simply making sure an aging parent isn't a financial burden on their adult children if something happens. By the time anyone thinks about it, age and health have often already closed off the cheapest, easiest options.

Why cost and insurability change so sharply with age

Life insurance pricing is driven heavily by age and health, and the relationship isn't linear — premiums rise more steeply in your 60s and 70s than they did in your 40s and 50s, and more health conditions accumulate with each passing year, narrowing which insurers will offer standard rates versus a rated premium versus nothing at all. Someone who could have locked in affordable coverage at 55 often finds the same coverage dramatically more expensive, or simply unavailable, by 70.

The situations we see most often

  • Parents sponsored for permanent residency who arrive in their 60s or later, often with no existing Canadian coverage and limited awareness of what's available here.
  • Parents or grandparents visiting on a Super Visa whose mandatory insurance covers emergency medical only — not final expenses or anything else.
  • Aging parents already living in Canada whose life insurance needs were never revisited after their working years ended, when the original reasons for coverage (income replacement, a mortgage) no longer applied but new reasons (final expenses, leaving something for grandchildren) emerged.

A real trade-off worth naming

Adult children sometimes assume an aging parent "doesn't need" life insurance because there's no mortgage or dependent children to protect. But final expenses, any debt the parent carries, and simply not wanting to be a financial burden on family during an already difficult time are all real, common reasons older adults still choose coverage — the need doesn't disappear, it just changes shape.

What still works, even later in life

  • Simplified issue policies are often still available well into a person's 70s, with a short health questionnaire rather than a full medical exam.
  • Guaranteed issue, small-face-value policies (see our final expense insurance guide) remain available regardless of health, specifically for this situation.
  • Acting sooner rather than later still matters even within "later in life" — coverage applied for at 68 is typically cheaper and easier to get than the same coverage applied for at 75.

The one thing worth doing regardless of age

If a parent or grandparent is in Canada on a Super Visa, their mandatory visitor insurance only covers emergency medical costs — not final expenses, not any debts, not anything beyond the medical emergency it's designed for. A small guaranteed-issue policy alongside that required coverage closes a gap most families don't realize exists until it's too late to address cheaply.

Frequently Asked

Insuring aging parents & grandparents, answered.

Can my elderly parent still get life insurance in Canada?+
In most cases, yes. Simplified issue policies are commonly available into a person's 70s, and guaranteed issue (final expense) policies remain available at any age within the insurer's eligible range, regardless of health.
Does Super Visa insurance cover final expenses?+
No — Super Visa insurance is specifically emergency medical coverage, required by IRCC as part of the visa application. It doesn't cover funeral costs, debts, or anything outside a medical emergency. A separate small life insurance policy is needed to cover those.
Why does life insurance get so much more expensive with age?+
Premiums are priced on mortality risk, which rises with age, and more health conditions tend to accumulate over time — both factors compound, especially from the 60s onward, which is why premiums rise more steeply later in life than earlier.
Is it worth buying life insurance for a parent who has no mortgage or dependents?+
Often yes — common reasons include covering final expenses, paying off any remaining debt, or simply ensuring adult children aren't left with unexpected costs during an already difficult time. The need doesn't disappear with age, it changes form.
What's the cheapest way to insure an aging parent?+
It depends on their health and how much coverage is needed. A simplified-issue policy is usually cheaper than guaranteed issue if the applicant qualifies, since guaranteed issue trades no health questions for a higher premium and a graded benefit period.
Should I buy insurance for a parent coming to Canada permanently or just visiting?+
Both situations benefit from a conversation — a parent immigrating permanently has different, longer-term needs than one visiting on a Super Visa, whose mandatory coverage is purely medical and temporary. The right product depends on their status and plans.
