Super Visa insurance: the rules, explained simply

By Rattan Amol Taggar Updated Oct 20, 2026 10 min read
Quick Summary
  • Super Visa insurance is mandatory, not optional — proof of it is a required part of the application.
  • Minimum coverage: $100,000 CAD in emergency medical coverage, valid for at least one full year from the date of entry.
  • Required benefits: health care, hospitalization, and repatriation.
  • Pricing for the same $100,000 minimum varies a lot by age and health — always compare multiple insurers.

What the Super Visa is

The Super Visa is a long-stay temporary resident visa for parents and grandparents of Canadian citizens or permanent residents. It allows longer stays per entry than a standard visitor visa, without needing to renew status annually. The applicant must be a parent or grandparent of a Canadian citizen or permanent resident, and the host in Canada typically needs to meet certain income requirements and provide a letter of invitation.

Insurance requirements, in full

Unlike general visitor insurance, mandatory private medical insurance is a hard requirement to qualify for a Super Visa — the application isn't approved without it.

RequirementMinimum
Coverage amount$100,000 CAD in emergency medical coverage
DurationAt least 1 full year from the date of entry to Canada
Required benefitsHealth care, hospitalization, and repatriation
IssuerA Canadian insurance company, or one meeting IRCC's approval requirements

The policy must be purchased for at least a full year even if the actual visit is shorter — though many insurers offer a partial refund for unused time if the visa holder returns home early, which is worth confirming with the specific insurer before buying.

Requirements can change — confirm before you buy

Specific income thresholds, approved-insurer lists, and program details can be updated. This article summarizes the general structure of the requirement, not a guarantee of current figures — always confirm current Super Visa requirements directly on the official IRCC (Immigration, Refugees and Citizenship Canada) website, or with your advisor, before purchasing.

Which insurers qualify

The insurance generally must be purchased from a Canadian insurance company, or one that meets IRCC's specific approval requirements. Because the list of qualifying insurers and the exact rules can be updated, confirm that your chosen insurer currently meets IRCC's requirements before you buy — don't assume a policy qualifies just because it meets the $100,000/one-year minimums on paper.

Buying tips: how to not overpay

  • Compare multiple insurers — pricing for the same $100,000 minimum varies significantly by the applicant's age and health history.
  • Consider the deductible — a higher deductible can meaningfully lower the premium; weigh that against the applicant's health and planned length of stay.
  • Buy before the flight — the policy typically needs to be in effect from the date of entry.
  • Ask about the refund policy upfront in case the visit ends earlier than planned.

Getting a refund if your parent leaves early

Most Super Visa insurance policies allow a prorated refund of unused premium if the insured person leaves Canada before the policy's term ends and no claim has been made — but the process and exact terms vary by insurer, and some charge a cancellation or administration fee. It's worth confirming the refund policy in writing before buying, especially for a full-year or multi-year policy, since the amounts involved can be significant.

Renewing or extending coverage

If your parent or grandparent's stay runs longer than planned, Super Visa insurance generally needs to be renewed or extended before the existing policy lapses — a gap in coverage, even briefly, can jeopardize their visa status, since IRCC requires valid insurance for the full duration of the stay. Renewal timing and whether a new medical declaration is required varies by insurer, so it's best to start that conversation well before the current policy's end date.

Frequently Asked

Super Visa insurance, answered.

Is Super Visa insurance mandatory?+
Yes — proof of private medical insurance meeting the minimum requirements is a mandatory part of the Super Visa application. It isn't optional like regular visitor insurance.
How much coverage does Super Visa insurance require?+
At least $100,000 CAD in emergency medical coverage.
How long does the policy need to last?+
At least one full year from the date of entry to Canada.
Does it have to be from a Canadian insurer?+
Generally yes, or an insurer meeting IRCC's specific approval requirements — always confirm the current list before buying, since this can change.
Can I get a refund if my parent returns home early?+
Many insurers offer a partial refund for unused time if the visa holder leaves Canada before the policy term ends — check the specific policy's refund terms before buying.
What's covered besides hospitalization?+
Health care and repatriation are required benefits alongside hospitalization.
Can I get a refund if my parent leaves Canada early?+
In most cases, yes — a prorated refund of unused premium is typically available if no claim has been made, though the process, timeline, and any cancellation fee vary by insurer. Confirm the refund terms in writing before buying.
What happens if my parent's visit runs longer than the insurance covers?+
The policy needs to be renewed or extended before it lapses — a coverage gap can jeopardize their visa status, since IRCC requires valid insurance for the full duration of the stay. Start the renewal process before the current policy's end date.
