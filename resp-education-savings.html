The RESP: free government money for your kids' education

By Rattan Amol Taggar Updated Oct 20, 2026 8 min read

How the RESP and CESG work

A Registered Education Savings Plan (RESP) is a tax-sheltered account for saving toward a child's post-secondary education. Contributions aren't tax-deductible, but all investment growth inside the account grows tax-deferred.

The real draw is the Canada Education Savings Grant (CESG): the federal government matches 20% of the first $2,500 contributed per child, per year — up to $500 per year in free grant money, for simply contributing to the account.

Limits & catch-up room

RuleAmount
Basic CESG match20% of the first $2,500/year
Annual CESG cap$500/year (or up to $1,000/year with catch-up room)
Lifetime CESG maximum$7,200 per child
Lifetime RESP contribution limit$50,000 per beneficiary

If you missed a year, catch-up room lets the CESG match up to $5,000 of contributions (at 20%) in a single year, for up to $1,000 in grant money — useful for parents who couldn't contribute consistently from birth.

You can contribute beyond what earns a grant (up to the $50,000 lifetime cap), but the government simply stops matching once the $7,200 lifetime CESG maximum is reached.

Extra help for lower-income families

Lower- and middle-income households may receive an additional 10% to 20% on the first $500 contributed annually, on top of the basic 20% — an effective match rate of 30–40% on that first $500/year for qualifying families.

There's also the Canada Learning Bond (CLB): an additional government contribution available to lower-income families, on top of the CESG, with no contribution required to receive it. It's worth confirming your eligibility and exact amount with your RESP provider or canada.ca, since it varies by number of children and family income.

A worked example

Starting early beats a lump sum

A parent contributing $2,500/year starting at birth receives the full $500 CESG match every year. By the time the child turns 18, on top of $45,000 in their own contributions (18 years × $2,500), they'd have received up to the full $7,200 lifetime CESG — plus all the investment growth on both. You don't need a lump sum to capture the match; consistent smaller contributions started early are what actually maximize the free grant money over time.

What if my child doesn't go?

The money isn't lost. Contributions can be withdrawn tax-free (since they were never tax-deductible). Investment growth (an Accumulated Income Payment) can sometimes be transferred to an RRSP, within your available RRSP room, to avoid tax — or withdrawn with tax plus an additional penalty if not transferred. CESG and other grant money generally has to be repaid to the government if it isn't used for education. There are structured options either way — it's worth planning around rather than worrying about.

Frequently Asked

RESP, answered.

How much does the government match in an RESP?+
20% of the first $2,500 contributed per child per year, up to $500/year, with a $7,200 lifetime maximum per child.
Is there a deadline to open an RESP?+
CESG eligibility generally ends the year the child turns 17, with specific rules for 16–17 year olds requiring a minimum contribution history — so starting earlier maximizes the grant captured.
What if my child doesn't go to post-secondary school?+
Contributions come back tax-free, investment growth has options including an RRSP transfer within room limits, and most CESG/grant money is returned to the government if unused — the account isn't simply lost.
Can grandparents or other family members contribute to an RESP?+
Yes — anyone can contribute to a child's RESP, though the lifetime $50,000 contribution limit per child applies across everyone contributing.
Do lower-income families get extra help?+
Yes — an additional CESG match on the first $500/year, plus the Canada Learning Bond, which can provide government contributions even without the family contributing anything.
When should I start an RESP?+
As early as possible — starting at birth maximizes the number of years available to capture the annual grant match before the eligibility cutoff.
