Visitor, Super Visa, or travel insurance — which one do you actually need?

By Rattan Amol Taggar Updated Oct 21, 2026 7 min read
Quick Summary
  • Visitor insurance covers anyone visiting Canada who isn't eligible for provincial health coverage — there's no fixed government minimum.
  • Super Visa insurance is a specific, mandatory type of visitor insurance for parents and grandparents: at least $100,000 CAD, valid for one year, from an approved Canadian insurer.
  • Travel insurance covers Canadian residents travelling outside Canada — the opposite direction from the other two.
  • The fastest way to tell them apart: which direction is the person travelling, and are they specifically on a Super Visa?

Why these three get mixed up constantly

"Travel insurance," "visitor insurance," and "Super Visa insurance" all sound like the same thing — coverage for someone who isn't a Canadian resident — and in a general sense they are. But each term describes a specific product with its own rules, and using the wrong one can mean a declined application, a denied claim, or coverage that doesn't actually match the situation.

Visitor to Canada insurance

Visitor insurance is the broad category: emergency medical coverage for anyone visiting Canada who isn't covered by a provincial health plan — tourists, visiting family members, international students waiting on a provincial plan, or temporary workers before their coverage kicks in. It pays for emergency medical treatment, hospitalization, and often prescription drugs and repatriation, for the length of the stay. See our full Visitor to Canada Insurance guide for coverage amounts and common mistakes.

Super Visa insurance

Super Visa insurance is a specific, government-mandated type of visitor insurance required only for parents and grandparents applying for a Super Visa — the long-stay visa (up to five years per entry) that lets them visit a Canadian citizen or permanent resident child or grandchild. IRCC sets hard minimums: at least $100,000 CAD in emergency medical coverage, valid for a minimum of one year from entry, purchased from a Canadian insurance company (or one on an approved list), and proof of payment is required as part of the visa application itself. Our Super Visa insurance guide covers the full requirements and how to compare eligible insurers.

General travel insurance

Travel insurance is the broadest term, and usually describes coverage for Canadian residents travelling outside the country — the opposite direction from the other two. It can include emergency medical, trip cancellation or interruption, lost baggage, and flight delay coverage. A Canadian heading to India for a family wedding needs travel insurance; their parent coming to Canada on a Super Visa needs Super Visa insurance — same family, two completely different policies.

Common mistakes we see

  • Buying generic visitor insurance for a Super Visa application that doesn't meet the $100,000 minimum or the one-year duration — this can get the visa application itself rejected, not just the insurance claim.
  • Assuming OHIP or another provincial plan covers visiting parents. It doesn't, regardless of how long they stay or whether they're related to a citizen or permanent resident.
  • Buying travel insurance (for Canadians abroad) when visitor insurance (for someone visiting Canada) is what's actually needed — these are sold by the same companies and can look identical on a quote page.
  • Letting coverage lapse mid-trip to save money, not realizing a new pre-existing condition exclusion period can apply if coverage is repurchased after a gap.

Quick way to tell which one you need

If the person travelling is coming to Canada and isn't eligible for provincial health coverage, it's visitor insurance — and if they're specifically a parent or grandparent on a Super Visa, it must meet the $100,000/one-year Super Visa minimums. If the person travelling is a Canadian resident going outside Canada, it's travel insurance. The direction of travel is the fastest way to tell them apart.

Frequently Asked

Visitor vs. Super Visa vs. travel insurance, answered.

What's the difference between visitor insurance and Super Visa insurance?+
Super Visa insurance is a specific type of visitor insurance that meets IRCC's mandatory requirements for the Super Visa program: at least $100,000 CAD in coverage, valid for a minimum of one year, from an approved Canadian insurer. General visitor insurance covers anyone visiting Canada but has no fixed government minimum — the right amount depends on the visit.
Do I need travel insurance if I already have visitor insurance?+
They cover opposite directions of travel. Visitor insurance covers someone visiting Canada; travel insurance covers a Canadian resident travelling elsewhere. If you're a Canadian citizen hosting a visiting parent and also planning your own trip abroad, you'd need both — one for each person.
Is Super Visa insurance mandatory?+
Yes. Proof of a qualifying insurance policy (minimum $100,000 CAD, one year, from a Canadian or approved insurer) is a required document in the Super Visa application itself — without it, the application won't be approved.
Can I use Super Visa insurance for a regular visitor visa instead?+
Yes — Super Visa insurance meets or exceeds what's typically needed for a standard visitor stay, so it works fine for a shorter visit too. The reverse isn't true: a basic visitor policy usually won't meet Super Visa minimums.
Does OHIP cover visitors to Ontario?+
No. OHIP coverage is restricted to Ontario residents who meet specific residency requirements — visitors, regardless of how long they stay or their relationship to a Canadian citizen or resident, are not eligible and need their own insurance.
What happens if a visitor's insurance doesn't meet the minimum coverage required?+
For a Super Visa application specifically, insufficient coverage can result in the visa application being refused. For a general visit, inadequate coverage simply means higher out-of-pocket exposure if a medical emergency happens and costs exceed the policy limit.
