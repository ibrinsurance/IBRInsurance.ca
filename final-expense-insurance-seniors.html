Final expense insurance: small coverage, one specific job

By Rattan Amol Taggar Updated Oct 21, 2026 7 min read
Quick Summary
  • Final expense insurance is a small, permanent policy (usually up to $35,000) built specifically to cover funeral, burial, or cremation costs.
  • It comes in two tiers: simplified issue (a short health questionnaire) and guaranteed issue (no health questions, but a 2-year graded benefit).
  • A healthy 40-year-old can expect to pay roughly $20–$25 a month — and the premium is locked for life once approved.
  • It's especially relevant for seniors, anyone declined elsewhere for health reasons, and parents or grandparents visiting on a Super Visa.

What final expense insurance actually is

Final expense insurance (also called funeral or burial insurance) is a small whole life policy, usually with a face value up to around $35,000, built for one job: covering the costs that show up immediately after someone dies — the funeral, burial or cremation, and any outstanding small bills — without leaving family members to pay out of pocket or wait months for an estate to be settled.

It's permanent coverage, so as long as premiums are paid it never expires and the price never increases, unlike a term policy that eventually ends. The trade-off for that simplicity is a modest coverage amount; this isn't meant to replace income or pay off a mortgage, just to handle the costs families face in the first days and weeks.

Who final expense insurance is actually for

  • Seniors who no longer have (or never had) income-replacement needs, but want funeral costs covered without burdening their children.
  • Anyone who's been declined for a larger term or permanent policy due to health, and needs a guaranteed-issue option instead.
  • Parents of clients on a Super Visa — a small permanent policy here in Canada, alongside required visitor medical coverage, fills a gap that visitor insurance (which only covers emergency medical, not final expenses) doesn't address.
  • Anyone without savings set aside specifically for end-of-life costs, who wants a fixed, guaranteed amount available immediately rather than relying on an estate that can take months to settle.

Guaranteed issue vs. simplified issue

Final expense policies typically come in two underwriting tiers:

TypeHealth questionsApproval speedPremium
Simplified issueA short health questionnaire, no medical examDaysLower, if you qualify
Guaranteed issueNone — approval isn't based on healthImmediateHigher, and often includes a 2-year graded benefit

Guaranteed issue policies are the safety net for people who've been declined elsewhere: you can't be turned down for health reasons, but most guaranteed issue policies include a graded death benefit for the first two years — if death occurs from natural causes during that window, beneficiaries typically receive a return of premiums paid plus a percentage, rather than the full face value. After the graded period, full coverage applies.

What it costs

As a rough benchmark, a healthy, non-smoking 40-year-old can expect to pay around $20–$25 a month for a simplified-issue policy; premiums rise meaningfully with age and health history, which is exactly why it's worth applying well before a health event forces you into the guaranteed-issue tier. Because this is permanent coverage, the premium you lock in at application is the premium you pay for life — it doesn't increase as you get older, unlike renewing term coverage.

Final expense vs. a small term policy

If you're still relatively young and healthy, a small term policy can sometimes cover the same funeral-cost goal for less money during your working years — but term eventually ends, and re-applying in your 60s or 70s at standard term rates (if you even still qualify) can cost far more than a final expense policy locked in earlier. Final expense makes the most sense once you're past the age or health profile where a fresh term policy is still cheap and easy to get, generally mid-60s onward, or any time coverage needs to be guaranteed regardless of health.

Frequently Asked

Final expense insurance, answered.

How much does final expense insurance cost in Canada?+
A healthy, non-smoking 40-year-old typically pays around $20 to $25 a month for a simplified-issue policy. Cost rises with age and health, which is why applying earlier, before a health event forces a guaranteed-issue application, usually locks in a lower lifetime premium.
Do I need a medical exam for final expense insurance?+
No. Final expense policies are either simplified issue (a short health questionnaire, no exam) or guaranteed issue (no health questions at all). Neither requires a medical exam, which is part of why approval is fast.
What is a graded death benefit?+
A feature common to guaranteed-issue policies where, if death occurs from natural causes within the first two years, beneficiaries receive a return of premiums paid plus a percentage rather than the full face value. After that period, the full death benefit applies regardless of cause.
Is final expense insurance the same as burial insurance?+
They're generally used interchangeably in Canada — both describe a small, permanent policy sized to cover funeral, burial or cremation costs and related final bills, rather than income replacement or debt payoff.
Can I get final expense insurance if I've already been declined for life insurance?+
Often yes. Guaranteed-issue final expense policies don't use health questions to decide approval, which makes them one of the few options still available after a decline elsewhere — though they typically cost more and include the graded benefit described above.
How much coverage do I actually need for a funeral?+
It depends heavily on the type of service, casket or cremation, and region — costs vary widely across Canada. A conversation about your specific wishes and local costs gives a far more accurate number than a flat rule of thumb.
